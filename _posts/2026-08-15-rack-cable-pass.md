The rack hit the point where every change meant tracing cables by hand, so this weekend was a full cable pass: everything out, everything labeled, everything back in with intent.

## The labeling scheme

Every cable now gets a flag label at both ends with a simple ID: role prefix, then a number. Power, data, and everything else are visually distinct at a glance, and the mapping from ID to what-connects-to-what lives in the private notebook — the labels themselves say nothing a photo of the rack shouldn't reveal.

The rule that matters: **no cable goes in without a label**. Schemes don't fail at creation; they fail at the first lazy add-on six weeks later.

## Ergonomics

Two small changes with outsized payoff:

- Service loops on everything that might ever slide out on rails. Obvious in hindsight; the sleds can now come fully forward without unplugging a thing.
- Color-coded velcro by bundle, cut to a standard length, so re-dressing after a change takes seconds and looks the same every time.

## The regret

I bought custom-length cables for the cleanest possible dressing, and it does look great — but the first hardware swap already wants one cable two inches longer than its custom run. Fixed lengths optimize for the rack you have, not the rack you're about to change. The next order goes back to standard lengths plus service loops, and the glamour shots will survive.

Total time: one long afternoon, one skinned knuckle, zero mystery cables remaining.
